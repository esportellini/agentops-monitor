# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Engineering, platform, security, and AI operations teams that monitor production agents, investigate executions, control risky tool use, review approvals, manage spend, and evaluate model quality.

## Product Purpose

AgentOps Monitor is an operations, governance, security, and evaluation control plane for AI agents. It gives operators one place to understand system health, find work needing attention, inspect traces, enforce policy, and compare quality and cost.

## Positioning

The product connects execution telemetry with active controls: traces, authoritative pricing, security findings, policy enforcement, human approvals, alerts, and evaluations share the same operational context.

## Operating Context

The product is a dense, desktop-priority operations console used during routine monitoring and incident investigation. Its reproducible Docker demo supplies real linked data across all core workflows.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Preserve existing API contracts, TanStack Query, AuthContext, and working feature semantics.
- Never invent unavailable metrics, trends, prices, or search capabilities.
- Treat unpriced model calls as incomplete cost data, never zero cost.
- Keep all existing user-facing routes functional and responsive.
- Next.js remains at its current major and minor version during this redesign.

## Brand Commitments

The product name is AgentOps Monitor. Its voice is precise, calm, direct, and technical. The product should feel trustworthy and engineered, without fintech, CRM, cyberpunk, generic admin-template, or marketing-site cues.

## Evidence on Hand

The repository includes tested implementations and a reproducible Docker demo for tracing, security and redaction, pricing, policies, approvals, alerts, and evaluations. No external brand imagery, customer claims, or benchmark claims are available and none should be fabricated.

## Product Principles

1. Put the next operational decision within immediate reach.
2. Preserve provenance and completeness in cost, security, and evaluation data.
3. Favor scanability and connected context over decorative summaries.
4. Use semantic color only when it conveys state, risk, or action.
5. Keep every control honest about what the backend can do.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic controls, labeled forms, sufficient contrast, reduced-motion support, and usable layouts from 390px upward are required.
